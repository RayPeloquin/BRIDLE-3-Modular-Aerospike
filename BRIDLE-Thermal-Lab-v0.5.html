Finite reserve · mathematical budget

Separate a cooling budget from coolant mass

For zero main cooling, I = ∫a(τ)dτ. Holding θ ≤ 1.50 for all future time requires I ≥ 5/3 in this fixed normalized model. The proposed exponential reserve has I = 1.25.

What this calculation establishes

The switch trades additional neighbor heating for a lower local peak in the specified single-module fault. The stated reserve suppresses that switch in the same case. Neither result establishes containment, an equal-mass improvement, or survival of the shared-loss case.

Equations, switch policy and numerical method
dθᵢ/dτ = qᵢ − aᵢθᵢ + Σⱼ kᵢⱼ(θⱼ − θᵢ), with kᵢⱼ = kⱼᵢ ≥ 0.

Each ring edge has one reciprocal conductance. With switching enabled, its hotter endpoint closes the contact at θ = 1.35 and opens it at 1.30. High conductance is 0.40. Switch delay, mechanical work, wear and contact dynamics are omitted.

For the dimensional reference: θ = (T − Tc)/ΔTref; τ = tH0/C; a = H/H0; k = K/H0; q = Q/(H0ΔTref). Equal capacities and a fixed common coolant reference are assumed.

This offline explorer uses fourth-order Runge–Kutta with a 0.01 normalized step and threshold-event splitting. The accompanying Python reference uses adaptive integration and independent analytic checks. Peaks are numerical estimates; all-time statements here apply only to the explicitly stated scalar model.

Requirements that still need physical evidence
  • Measure heat capacities, local cooling behavior and seam/frame conductances. One steady-state temperature ratio cannot identify an arbitrary nonuniform network.
  • Define independent supply paths and shared failure modes. A/B labels and nominal 50/50 flow do not establish surviving local conductance.
  • Model finite coolant inventory, pressure, temperature, valve delivery, fluid storage and depletion.
  • Test switch delay, stuck-open/stuck-closed faults, thermal growth, seal leakage and shared frame paths.
  • Establish heat input from the coupled plume and wall model; preserve solid conduction as well.
  • Compare mass, stored energy, coolant inventory and pumping resources consistently before claiming an enlarged operating envelope.
